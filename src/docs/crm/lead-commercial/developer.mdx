The commercial end is **reads over existing columns** plus a handful of actions. There is no negotiation table: the Negotiation desk reads `customers` on the `negotiation` rung and its newest transition. Commitments are three `lead_expected_order_*` columns judged by one pure file, `lib/lead-commitment.ts`. Distributor appointments are `distributor_profiles` plus two `mbos_approvals` rows. Everything below is cut from the source this deployment was built from.

## Changing it safely

<Steps>
<Step title="Never add forecast to fact">
Anything named `forecast…` or read from `lead_expected_order_*` stays in its own total. A screen that sums the two is the bug this file was written to prevent.
</Step>
<Step title="Change what a commitment is in lead-commitment.ts only">
The record panel, the three desks and the confirm action all read it; the SQL string and the TypeScript function must change together.
</Step>
<Step title="Never write lead_stage from an order action">
Rungs that read the ledger (`first_order`, `delivery`, `payment`, `second_order`) move through the gate when the ledger says so.
</Step>
<Step title="Routing is pure">
A new reason to escalate goes in `approvalRouteReason`, which the handset, the console and the action all read.
</Step>
</Steps>
