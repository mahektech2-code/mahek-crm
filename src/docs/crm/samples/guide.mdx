Samples & trials is where a sample's whole life is worked: somebody asks for a can to give a shop, a manager says yes or no, it is sent, the shop confirms it arrived, they try it, and somebody writes down what they thought. **A sample with no feedback is stock given away**, so every screen here is built around getting that answer.

You reach it from **Lead Management → Samples & trials** in the CRM sidebar (it is also linked under *Other desks* on All Leads). The same four tabs exist on the Sales Dashboard; the screens are identical, only who you see differs — see [Whose samples you see](#whose-samples-you-see).

<Callout kind="live">
The numbers on this page — which days a review is chased on, how long before a parcel is chased — are the settings your CRM is using right now, read live.
</Callout>

## The four tabs

| Tab | What it answers | You use it to |
|---|---|---|
| **All samples** | What is out with customers, and which have no feedback yet | See what is late and who owes an answer |
| **Desk** | Where each sample has got to, and what it is owed next | Approve, record dispatch, confirm receipt, record the review, call it off |
| **Review chases** | Which trials are waiting on an answer, and how many times we have asked | Find the shops to ring yourself |
| **Trial feedback** | What every trial said, across products and competitors | Read patterns — "price was the problem in nine of eleven" |

Click any sample's customer to open the lead; open a sample itself to see its full history on the **sample record**.

## A sample, step by step

<Steps>
<Step title="Somebody asks for one">
A sample is asked for from the lead record (**Ask for a sample**), from the call assistant, or by a salesman on the handset. You give the **product**, how many **cans**, **what it will be used on**, and **why** they want one (a choice from a list such as *Wants to compare it with another brand*).

For a lead on the Direct or Third-party ladder, a sample can only be asked for once its **Qualification is complete and the Sales Manager has verified it**. If not, you are told exactly what is still to do and nothing is created. For an older lead, a sample asked for before the lead is Qualified is allowed but marked **"Lead not qualified — Manager approval required."** so the person approving it knows.
</Step>
<Step title="A manager approves or refuses">
On the desk, press **Approve** or **Refuse**. A refusal **must say why** — *"Say why — the salesman cannot work it out from the word alone."* The salesman's handset hears the answer. Only managers and admins can make this decision.
</Step>
<Step title="Record the dispatch">
Press **Record dispatch** and give the **courier**, the **docket number** and the **date they promised** delivery. All three are required: a docket nobody can chase is a sample nobody will review, and the promised date is what the late flag is measured against.
</Step>
<Step title="Confirm it was received">
When **the shop** says they have it, press **Confirm received** → **They have it**. This is the shop's word — not the courier's website. It starts the review clock.
</Step>
<Step title="Record what they thought">
Press **Record feedback** and fill in the seven questions (below), then pick the verdict: **Approved**, **Rejected** or **More testing**. At least one answer is required, and a rejection must say why.
</Step>
<Step title="Or call it off">
At any point before the review, **Call off** ends the trial. Pick one of the reasons; *Other* needs a sentence.
</Step>
</Steps>

## What each state means

| State on the screen | Stored as | Meaning | What it is owed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asked for | `requested` | Somebody asked; nobody has decided | A manager's yes or no |
| Approved | `approved` | Yes — it can go out | Dispatch |
| Refused | `rejected` | A manager said no, with a reason | Nothing. *Refused — nothing to send* |
| Sent | `dispatched` | It has left us, with a courier and docket | The shop confirming receipt |
| Delivered | `received` | The shop says they have it | Their review |
| Tried | `trial_done` | They have used it | Their review |
| Reviewed | `reviewed` | Their verdict is written down | Nothing — the trial is closed |
| Cancelled | `cancelled` | Called off, with a coded reason | Nothing |

<Callout kind="note" title="State and verdict are two different things">
The **state** is where the sample has got to. The **verdict** is what the customer thought of it — *Awaiting feedback*, *Approved*, *Rejected* or *More testing*. "Approved" in the State column means a **manager** said it may go out; "Approved" as a verdict means the **customer** liked it. The All samples list shows the verdict; the Desk shows the state.
</Callout>

## The three dates

The sample record shows three dates side by side, because three different people say three different things:

| Date | Who says it | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **It left us** | We say | The day it was dispatched |
| **It arrived** | The carrier says | Delivery, as reported by the courier or our own man |
| **It is in our hands** | **The shop** says | The confirmed receipt — the only date the review is counted from |

Confirming receipt on the desk records only the third date. A sample that sat in a courier office for four days and one handed over the same afternoon must not look the same.

## The seven feedback questions

| Question | What to write |
|---|---|
| **Quality** | What they thought of the product itself |
| **Performance** | How it performed in use |
| **Application** | How it went on — spraying, brushing, coverage |
| **Drying** | Drying time against what they expected |
| **Compared with what they use now** | The comparison against their current brand — the whole point of a trial |
| **Price** | What they said about the price |
| **Anything else** | Anything that does not fit above |

<Rule title="A rejection needs a reason">
If the verdict is **Rejected**, at least one of *Anything else*, *Price* or *Compared with what they use now* must be filled in. Otherwise you will see: *"A rejected trial has to say why — the price, the comparison against what they use now, or in your own words. Without it the next sample goes out exactly the same."*
</Rule>

The review is recorded **once**. A second opinion is a second sample.

### What each verdict does

<Cards cols={3}>
<Card title="Approved" tone="brand">The trial is closed and an **"Call about the order"** task goes to the lead manager. When the verdict comes from the handset, the lead is also moved up to **Negotiation** if its checklist allows.</Card>
<Card title="Rejected" tone="warn">The trial is closed with the reason on the record. The salesman and the lead manager are told why.</Card>
<Card title="More testing">They want to try it again — neither yes nor no. The lead stays where it is.</Card>
</Cards>

The review is chased on the days in <Setting k="leads.sampleReviewChaseDays" /> **after the shop confirmed receipt**, and after the last of those the last gap **repeats** — it never stops until somebody records the verdict or calls the sample off. Each ask becomes a task for the lead manager (or the salesman, where there is no lead manager), and the third ask onward is marked high priority.

A sample with **no confirmed receipt** cannot be chased — the screen lists those separately and tells you to confirm receipt on the desk.

## Trial feedback

The **Trial feedback** tab is a library: every trial's seven answers, filterable by **product**, **competitor** and **verdict**. The verdict counts follow your filters, so "of the trials against this competitor, how many did we lose" is one click. It writes nothing.

At the bottom, **Trials called off** counts every cancelled sample by reason, grouped by **whose problem** it was:

| Group | Reasons | Whose to fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Supply** | Product not available · Wrong product or wrong need | We could not give them the right stock |
| **Customer** | No longer needs it · Delayed the trial · Sample not needed any more · Not replying | The shop stopped replying, delayed, or does not want it now |
| **Sales** | Price or terms problem | The talk about price and terms stopped it |
| (on its own) | Other | Always needs a sentence |

## Parcels that do not arrive

- When a salesman records a dispatch from the handset, a task **"Where is the sample — Customer A"** is raised <Setting k="mbos.samples.transitChaseAfterDays" /> days after dispatch for the account's **back office person**. If the account has none, it goes to the **lead manager**, and the task says so: *"This is on your list because no back office person is named on this account — naming one sends the next chase to them."*
- Once a dispatched sample passes the date the courier promised, a **"Sample has not arrived"** task is raised once for the lead manager (or the salesman).
- Confirming receipt **closes** those chases as done — it does not delete them, so the record still shows the parcel was slow.

## Whose samples you see

| Screen | You see |
|---|---|
| All samples, Desk | As a telecaller or salesman: the samples **you asked for**. As a manager: your region's salesmen, or every sample if no region has been allocated to you. |
| Review chases, sample record | Samples on customers in your book (your own as a telecaller, your team's as a manager). |

## Questions people ask

<Faq q="I pressed Approve on All samples and it said to use the samples desk.">
Decide sample requests on the **Desk** tab. The approval there checks the stock and the lead; the button on All samples cannot decide a sample.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Why can't I ask for a sample for this lead?">
On the Direct and Third-party ladders a sample needs the lead's Qualification complete and verified by the Sales Manager. The message lists exactly what is still missing — finish those and ask again.
</Faq>
<Faq q="The review call is not on anybody's list.">
The chase counts from the day **the shop** confirmed receipt. If nobody pressed **Confirm received**, there is no date to count from. Confirm it on the desk.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Can I change the review after saving it?">
No. The verdict is recorded once. If they want to try again, that is a new sample.
</Faq>
<Faq q="The sample arrived, but the shop says it was the wrong one.">
A sample cannot go back a step. Call it off with **Wrong product or wrong need** and ask for another.
</Faq>
<Faq q="What does 'Asked 4 times' mean for me as a manager?">
The automatic chase has asked four times and nobody has an answer. Ring the shop yourself.
</Faq>
