Customers is the list of every account we **invoice or deliver to** in your book, with the figures you need mid-call, and the door to each customer's **record** — one page with everything about them: who they are, who looks after them, what they buy, what they owe, and every call, order, bill and message.

Leads are not on this list. A lead is an account that has never ordered, and it is worked in Lead Management.

## Three kinds of account

| Type | Means |
|---|---|
| **Direct customer** | We sell to them and we invoice them. |
| **Third-party customer** | A shop we **deliver** to but do not bill — a distributor buys the goods and sells them on. Never asked for a first order on the Call Log. |
| **Lead** | Has never ordered. Not on this list; open it from Lead Management. |

The type filter also offers two working lists: **Delivered to on another's bill** — accounts the order sheet shows receiving goods somebody else was invoiced for, whether or not anybody has converted them yet — and **Third party, nobody billing**, which should be empty.

## Status words

| Status | Means |
|---|---|
| **Active** | Buying |
| **New** | Has never ordered |
| **Slow payer** | Has paid several bills well past their due date recently |
| **Inactive** | Has gone well past their own buying cycle without an order |
| **Deactivated** | Closed deliberately by a manager |

## Changing who looks after an account

<Steps>
<Step title="Tick the accounts, or open one record">
On the list, tick rows and press **Update account manager**. On a record, use the Account managers card.
</Step>
<Step title="Pick the new sales and/or back-office person">
Leave a seat untouched to keep it as it is. *Unassigned* empties it.
</Step>
<Step title="Say why">
Pick a reason from the list. It is kept with the change.
</Step>
<Step title="Save">
Both the person who gains the accounts and the person who loses them are told — once each, not once per account.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="note">
Moving the **sales** or **back-office** seat is an accounts or admin action, because it moves whose numbers the account counts towards. The **sales manager** — who the salesperson answers to — can be set by a CRM manager.
</Callout>

### Moving a whole book of sales managers

When somebody leaves, filter the list to everything they managed, press **Transfer sales manager**, pick the new person and confirm. The accounts that move are exactly the accounts the filters match. If the count changed between your review and the save — say an import landed — it stops and asks you to look again. With no filters at all it says, in red, that this is the whole book.

## Converting a shop to third party

From the row menu or the record: **Convert to third-party customer**, then name the distributor who bills them. A shop cannot be marked third party without somebody billing it. **No longer a third-party customer** undoes it and keeps the record of who used to bill them. An account we invoice ourselves cannot be converted.

## Questions people ask

<Faq q="Where are the leads?">
In Lead Management. The customers list is accounts we invoice or deliver to.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Why is the Update account manager button greyed out?">
Moving the sales or back-office seat is an accounts or admin action. The button says so when you hover it.
</Faq>
<Faq q="I changed the salesperson and the old name came back.">
A change made here is marked as a decision and the sheet sync keeps its hands off it. If the old name came back, check whether the change saved — the history on the record lists every move.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Export only gave me 25 customers.">
Export writes the rows on the page you are looking at. Raise the page size, or ask accounts for a full extract.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Next call is blank for most customers.">
It shows what the last call said would happen next. A customer nobody has called yet has nothing to show.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Why does the Activity tab say 1,204 but show ten?">
The counts are the whole history; the list loads ten at a time so the page stays fast. Press **Load older**, or pick a kind.
</Faq>
