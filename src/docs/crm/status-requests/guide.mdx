Close/Reopen is where requests to **close** a customer account, or to **bring a closed one back**, wait for a manager's answer. A telecaller asks; a manager decides. Nothing on this list has changed a customer's status yet — a request only changes something when a manager approves it.

There are two halves to this page. If you are a **telecaller**, you only ever ask — from the Call Log or from the customer list — and you never see the Close/Reopen screen itself. If you are a **manager**, Close/Reopen is your queue: one list for both directions, oldest first.

## Asking for an account to be closed

You find out a shop has shut, a record is a duplicate, or somebody on the list is not a customer at all. You cannot close the account yourself — you ask, with a reason.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the row menu (⋯)">
On the **Call Log**, open the menu on the customer's row and choose **Request deactivation**. On the **Customers** list, open the row menu and choose **Request deactivation**, or tick several customers and press **Request deactivation** in the bar that appears at the bottom.
</Step>
<Step title="Say why">
A window asks *Request deactivation for Customer A?* and will not let you continue without a reason. Write what a manager needs to decide: "Shop closed in August — owner confirmed on the phone", "Duplicate of Customer B". The manager decides on your words, so make them specific.
</Step>
<Step title="Press Request deactivation">
You see *Deactivation requested - a manager decides*. Every manager is told.
</Step>
<Step title="Carry on as normal">
The customer **stays visible and stays on your list** until a manager approves. The menu item now reads **Deactivation already requested** and is greyed out — hovering it says *Asked for already - a manager has yet to decide*. On the Customers list the row carries a <Pill tone="warn">Deactivation asked</Pill> badge.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="warn" title="Ticking several customers at once">
A bulk request goes through only if **every** ticked customer is in your book. If even one belongs to somebody else, the whole request is refused with *Some of those customers are not in your book.* — nothing is flagged. Untick the ones that are not yours and try again.
</Callout>

## Asking for a closed account to be reopened

A closed customer is off every calling list, and an order from them does **not** reopen the account by itself. Somebody has to ask.

<Steps>
<Step title="Find them on the Customers list">
Filter the status to **Deactivated** to see closed accounts.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose Request reactivation">
From the row menu, or tick the customers and press **Request reactivation** in the bottom bar. The bar only offers *reactivation* when **every** ticked customer is deactivated; a mixed selection offers *Request deactivation* instead.
</Step>
<Step title="Say what has changed">
The window reads *Ask to bring back Customer A?* — "The reason is what the manager decides on, so say what has changed." Then press **Request reactivation**.
</Step>
<Step title="Wait for the decision">
The row shows <Pill tone="brand">Reactivation asked</Pill> and the menu item reads **Reactivation already asked for**. The customer stays off every list until a manager approves.
</Step>
</Steps>

### Reading a row

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Customer** | The account name (click it to open the record), and underneath, whose book it is in — or *Nobody assigned*. |
| **Asked for** | <Pill tone="danger">Close the account</Pill> or <Pill tone="success">Bring it back</Pill>. |
| **Reason** | What the person who asked wrote. *No reason recorded* on very old requests. |
| **Asked by** | Who asked, and the day. *Not recorded* / *date not recorded* on requests raised before the system stored the asker. |
| **Outstanding** | What they still owe, highlighted when it is more than zero. |
| **Last order** | The date of their last order, or *never*. |

The list is sorted **oldest first** — the longest wait is the one somebody has been waiting on. Requests with no recorded date sit at the bottom.

### Deciding a request

<Steps>
<Step title="Press Approve or Reject">
A confirmation opens. Its wording depends on what you are doing.
</Step>
<Step title="Read what it says will happen">
**Approving a close:** *Customer A is marked deactivated. Nothing is deleted — every call, order and bill stays queryable — but they leave the calling queue, the collections worklist and the inactive watch.* If they owe money, it adds that the debt is **not written off** but nobody will be prompted to chase it. If they ordered in the last month, it says so.

**Approving a reopen:** *Customer D goes back to active and returns to the calling queue on their own buying cycle. The deactivation reason on the record is cleared.*

**Rejecting either:** *Nothing changes about Customer A. The request leaves this list, and whoever asked will need to be told why.*
</Step>
<Step title="Confirm">
Press **Yes, do it** (or **Reject the request**). You see *Customer deactivated*, *Customer brought back* or *Request rejected*, and the row leaves the list.
</Step>
<Step title="Tell the person who asked">
The system does **not** notify them of your decision. If you rejected it, ring or message them.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="note">
You can also decide from the **Customers** list: on a row with a pending request, a manager's menu shows **Approve deactivation** / **Reject the request** (or **Approve reactivation** / **Reject the request**) instead of the request item. It is the same decision.
</Callout>

## What closing an account does — and does not do

<Cards cols={2}>
<Card title="It does" tone="warn">
- Marks the customer **Deactivated**.
- Takes them off the **Call Log**, the **Payment Follow-up** worklist and the **inactive watch**.
- Records who closed it, when, and why.
- Stops the order sheet changing this account's status from now on.
</Card>
<Card title="It does not" tone="success">
- Delete anything. Every call, order, bill and payment stays on the record.
- Write off what they owe. The bills and the balance stay.
- Reopen itself if they order again — only a manager approving a reopen does that.
</Card>
</Cards>

## Statuses you will see

| Status | Who sets it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| <Pill tone="success">Active</Pill> | The system | Buying, or not yet quiet for long enough to worry about. |
| <Pill tone="warn">Inactive</Pill> | The system, every night | Gone quiet for <Setting k="inactive.cycleMultiplier" />× their own buying cycle. An order puts them back to Active on its own. |
| <Pill>Deactivated</Pill> | **A manager**, here | Closed on purpose. Nothing automatic ever sets or clears it. |

## Questions people ask

<Faq q="I asked for a customer to be closed. Why are they still on my Call Log?">
Asking does not close anything. They stay on your list until a manager approves. If you do not want to ring them today, use **Skip for today** with a reason.
</Faq>
<Faq q="The Request deactivation item is greyed out.">
Somebody — maybe you — has already asked, and no manager has decided yet. Hover it to see *Asked for already - a manager has yet to decide*.
</Faq>
<Faq q="A closed customer has just placed an order. Are they back?">
No. An order never reopens a deactivated account; that was somebody's decision. Ask for **Request reactivation** on the Customers list and a manager will decide.
</Faq>
<Faq q="I can't see Close/Reopen in my sidebar.">
Only people who can decide requests see it — CRM and Sales managers and administrators. A telecaller asks from the Call Log or Customers list and never needs the screen.
</Faq>
<Faq q="My request disappeared from the list but the customer is not closed.">
A manager rejected it. The reason you wrote was cleared with it, and you are not notified — ask your manager why.
</Faq>
<Faq q="The customer owes money. Should I still ask for them to be closed?">
You can, but say so in the reason. The manager sees the balance on the screen, and closing the account means nobody will be prompted to chase it.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Will the spreadsheet reopen a customer I closed?">
No. Once a manager has decided a customer's status here — either way — the order sheet's customer master stops speaking for that account's status.
</Faq>
