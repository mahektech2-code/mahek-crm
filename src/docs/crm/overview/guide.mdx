The CRM is where the office team rings customers, takes orders over the phone, chases money that is owed, and keeps a record of every conversation. It is one app inside MahekOne: you sign in once, and if the CRM is the only app you have been given, you land straight in it.

This page is the map. It tells you who the CRM is for, what a normal day looks like, what every screen in the sidebar is for, and what the words the CRM uses actually mean. Each screen has its own page with the details — follow the links.

## Who uses the CRM

<Cards cols={3}>
<Card title="Telecaller" tone="brand">Rings customers from the **Call Log** every day, logs what happened on each call, takes orders, sets callbacks and chases payments. Sees **only their own book** of customers.</Card>
<Card title="Manager">Runs a team of telecallers. Sees the **whole team** by default and can switch to their own book. Reads the team overview, sets targets, decides close/reopen requests and can close a reminder by hand.</Card>
<Card title="Accounts">The ledger desk mostly works in the Accounts app. Somebody on that desk who has **also** been given the CRM reads customers, bills and outstanding here, and approves orders back in Accounts.</Card>
</Cards>

What you can open inside the CRM is decided by your manager on the Access screen. If a screen in this guide is missing from your sidebar, you have not been given it — ask your manager.

## A telecaller's day

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the Dashboard">
It greets you by name and shows the shape of your day as five steps: **Work the queue → Close reminders → Chase payments → Clear complaints → Submit EOD**. Each step is ticked when there is nothing left in it. Press **Start calling** to begin. See [Dashboard](/docs/crm/dashboard).
</Step>
<Step title="Work the Call Log from the top">
The [Call Log](/docs/crm/call-log) lists today's customers in the order you should ring them, each with the reason. Call, log what happened, move to the next row.
</Step>
<Step title="Keep your promises">
Any callback you promised shows on [Reminders](/docs/crm/reminders) and jumps to the top of the Call Log on the day it is due. Making the call closes it.
</Step>
<Step title="Chase money">
[Payment Follow-up](/docs/crm/payments) is the collections list: who owes money, how late, and what was last promised.
</Step>
<Step title="Deal with complaints">
Open [Complaints](/docs/crm/complaints) and mention any open complaint before anything else on the next call to that customer.
</Step>
<Step title="Submit your EOD report">
At the end of the day, the [EOD Report](/docs/crm/eod) adds up what you did. It will not let you submit while a reminder due today or earlier is still open.
</Step>
</Steps>

## A manager's day

<Steps>
<Step title="Read the team overview">
The Dashboard shows **Team overview** instead of a greeting: today's red flags, the team's calls, orders booked, outstanding, and a comparison table of every telecaller. Switch the period to Yesterday, This week or This month.
</Step>
<Step title="Look for what is stuck">
Overdue reminders by telecaller (on [Reminders](/docs/crm/reminders)), opportunities past their expected day (on [Opportunities](/docs/crm/opportunities)), and **Unassigned** rows on the team Call Log.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide what is waiting for you">
Requests to close or reopen an account are on [Close/Reopen](/docs/crm/status-requests). Monthly targets are on [Monthly Targets](/docs/crm/targets).
</Step>
<Step title="Switch between team and your own book">
The **My book / Team** switch in the header changes every list in the CRM at once. It is a view, not a permission — it never lets anyone see more than they are allowed.
</Step>
</Steps>

## How the screens fit together

Almost everything in the CRM starts with a call and ends with the CRM telling you when that customer comes back.

<Flow caption="The main loop of the CRM" chart={`
flowchart TD
  A["Call Log<br/>who to ring today, and why"] --> B["Call panel<br/>log the call and its outcome"]
  R["Reminders<br/>promises falling due"] --> A
  B --> O["Order<br/>waits for Accounts"]
  B --> M["Reminder<br/>a callback or promise"]
  B --> C["Complaint"]
  B --> P["Payment promised<br/>or reported"]
  B --> Q["Opportunity<br/>something they may buy"]
  O --> AC["Accounts approve<br/>or decline it"]
  P --> AC2["Accounts confirm<br/>the money in the bank"]
  M --> R
  B --> N["Next step<br/>'Comes back to your Call Log on …'"]
  N --> A
`} />

A few things to know about this loop:

- **An order you take is not yet a sale.** It waits for the accounts desk to approve it. Your Dashboard counts it as *taken* straight away; the money only counts once it is approved.
- **A payment the customer says they made is not yet money.** It waits for accounts to find it in the bank. Until then the customer is left alone for a few days rather than chased.
- **Every save tells you when the customer comes back.** That sentence is kept on the call, so [Call History](/docs/crm/history) can show you later what you were told.

## Words the CRM uses

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Book** | The customers that are yours. A lead belongs to whoever owns it; a customer belongs to their sales account manager. Every list in the CRM is cut to a book. |
| **Scope** | Whose book you are looking at — **your book** or **the whole team**. A telecaller is always on their own book. A manager can switch. |
| **Held back** | A customer the Call Log deliberately left off today's list. The held-back strip says why for each one — nobody disappears silently. |
| **Buying cycle** | How many days a customer usually goes between orders, worked out from their own approved orders. The Call Log times its calls from it. |
| **Stock check** | A call partway through a customer's buying cycle to ask what is left on the shelf. |
| **Stage floor** | On a late payment, the lowest follow-up stage a customer can be at. A customer who refuses to commit or cannot be reached is kept at a higher stage even if their bill dates alone would put them lower. |
| **Reported money** | A payment the customer *says* they made. It stops the chasing for a few days but does not reduce what they owe. |
| **Confirmed money** | A payment accounts have found in the bank. Only this reduces outstanding. |
| **Lead** | An account that has never ordered. |
| **Customer** | An account we have invoiced — they have placed an order. |
| **Third-party customer** | A shop we deliver to but do not bill; a distributor buys from us and sells to them. The CRM never asks them for a first order. |
| **Reminder** | A promise somebody made to a customer — a callback, a payment to check, information to send. |
| **Opportunity** | Something a customer said on a call they might buy. It is not a lead. |
| **Next step** | The sentence you see after saving a call — *Comes back to your Call Log on …* — and the badge saying whether it is a promise or a scheduled date. |

## Questions people ask

<Faq q="I cannot see a screen my colleague can see.">
Your manager has given you a narrower set of screens. The Access screen decides it per person. Typing the address of a screen you were not given takes you to the first screen you do have.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Why does my manager see more customers than I do?">
A manager sees their whole team's books. A telecaller always sees only their own. The **My book / Team** switch is only drawn for managers.
</Faq>
<Faq q="I took an order but my Dashboard says it is 'awaiting approval'.">
Orders wait for the accounts desk to approve them. It counts as **taken** at once; it counts as money only once approved.
</Faq>
<Faq q="The customer says they paid. Why do they still show as owing?">
Money a customer reports is not counted until accounts confirm it reached the bank. Until then the customer is held back from chasing for a few days, and the screens say so.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Where do I change a setting like how many days of quiet after an order?">
Settings are changed by a manager in the Admin Console. The Help Center has a link for managers.
</Faq>
