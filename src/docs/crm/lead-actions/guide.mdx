Every active lead should owe somebody something: **what happens next, on which day, who does it, and what they should come back with**. The **Next actions & nurture** desk shows those promises across the whole book — what is due, what is late, what nobody has promised at all — and the follow-up tasks the system raises on its own. **Handovers** is where a lead that has become a customer is given to the person who will run the relationship from now on.

<Callout kind="live">
Numbers on this page are your CRM's current settings.
</Callout>

## Next actions & nurture: the six tabs

| Tab | What it lists |
|---|---|
| **Due** | Next actions falling due today, and who owes each one. |
| **Overdue** | Next actions past their day with nobody having said anything since. |
| **Nothing scheduled** | Active leads with **no** next action at all — the thing this desk exists to stop. |
| **On hold** | Parked leads, the day each comes back, and how far past that day it is. |
| **Nurture** | The follow-up tasks the system raised, split by whose they are — the lead manager's or the salesman's. |
| **Communication** | Every brochure, profile, price list and call recorded against leads, across the book. |

## Setting a next action

<Steps>
<Step title="Press Set the next action">
On the row here, or on the lead's own record.
</Step>
<Step title="Give all four answers">
**What** happens next, **which day** and **who** will do it are required; the person must be able to sign in. **What they should come back with** is optional but worth writing. If somebody else owes it, they are told.
</Step>
<Step title="Save">
The lead drops off *Nothing scheduled* and appears on *Due* on that day. Moving a lead up its ladder also asks for a next action while the rule is on (<Setting k="leads.requireNextAction" />), so most are set as part of a move.
</Step>
</Steps>

| Answer | Example |
|---|---|
| The action | Ring the works manager about the trial |
| The day | A date, never "soon" |
| Who | A person who can sign in |
| What they come back with (optional) | Whether the trial ran, and the result |

## Nurture tasks

When something happens on a lead, the system puts a dated task on somebody's list. You do not raise these yourself; you **do** them, and close them from your task list.

| When | After | Whose | Task |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead becomes a Prospect | same day | Lead manager | Check the customer and the salesman |
| Lead becomes a Prospect | same day | Salesman | Get and check GST |
| A salesman visits | 1 day | Lead manager | Check call after the visit |
| Lead becomes a Prospect | 2 days | Lead manager | Send the company profile |
| Lead becomes a Prospect | 4 days | Lead manager | Send the product brochure and video |
| A sample is asked for | same day | Lead manager | Check the sample request |
| A sample is dispatched | 2 days | Lead manager | Check it is moving |
| The shop confirms the sample | first day of the review ladder | Lead manager | Sample review call |
| The sample is approved | same day | Lead manager | Call about the order |
| Lead reaches Negotiation | 3 days | Lead manager | Follow up the negotiation |
| Their expected order date | that day | Lead manager | Ask for the first order |
| An order arrives | 1 day | Lead manager | Follow the delivery |
| Delivery completed | 2 days | Lead manager | Satisfaction call |
| A payment falls due | that day | Lead manager | Payment follow-up |
| Their reorder is expected | that day | Lead manager | Repeat-order call |

<Callout kind="note">
A task is only raised if there is somebody to give it to. A lead with no lead manager gets no lead-manager tasks — it shows up on the lists of leads with nobody running them instead. Each task is raised **once**, however many times the system checks.
</Callout>

## Communication

Record what went out to a lead: **Call the customer**, **Send the company profile**, **Send a product image**, **Send the brochure**, **Send the video**, **Send the price list**, **Sample follow-up**, **Negotiation call**, **Ask for the first order**, **Payment follow-up**, **Repeat-order call**. Press **Record the send** (or **Log the call**). The entry goes on the lead's timeline. The tab shows the log across the book and which of the eleven are used and which are never used.

### What a handover changes — and what it does not

| Changes | Does not change |
|---|---|
| Who runs the relationship, and the date it changed hands | Whose book the account is in |
| Who can **see** and work the account | Who is credited for its orders |
| A history row with the reason, and a timeline entry | Anybody's target, collections list or Call Log |

Both the person taking the account and the person losing it are told — one message each, however many accounts moved.

## Questions people ask

<Faq q="Why is a lead on Nothing scheduled when it has a follow-up date?">
A date is not a next action. It needs what, who and what they should come back with as well.
</Faq>
<Faq q="I did not create this nurture task. Where did it come from?">
Something happened on the lead — a sample went out, a visit was made, an order date arrived — and the sequence raised it. The task names the lead.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Can I hand over a lead?">
No. A lead is run by its lead manager until it becomes a customer. The Hand over button explains this.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Will handing over an account move its revenue to the new person?">
No. Moving revenue is a reassignment, which only accounts and administrators can do.
</Faq>
