WhatsApp is how most payment reminders reach customers and how most customers answer. The rules on this screen exist to keep one thing honest: **whether a customer has actually heard from us**. That decides whether the Call Log holds them back, whether collections counts a reminder as sent, and what a manager reads about the day.

## Read, and reply-and-send, are two grants

<Rule title="Reading the chats and writing to customers are separate">
The screen is one module; **reply and send** is a second module beside it. Somebody given only the first can open every chat and the message log, and nothing else. The server checks the level on every write — a reply box that is not drawn is not a permission.
</Rule>

| Level | Can |
|---|---|
| Read | Read chats and the message log |
| Reply and send | Reply in a chat, mark replies handled, send templates, work send runs, edit templates |

Sending a reminder from **other** screens — the payment follow-up panel, for example — is refused only for somebody deliberately narrowed to **read**. People who were never given the WhatsApp screen at all could always send a reminder from those screens, and still can.

<Callout kind="why" title="Manual stays fully usable">
Automatic sending is an addition, never a replacement. A deployment with sending switched off works entirely by copy, paste and confirm. A group cannot be reached through the API at all, and an edited message is no longer the approved wording WhatsApp allows a business to send.
</Callout>

<Rule title="A message that would read badly is refused">
Every merge field the template uses must have a value for this customer. "Dear ," never reaches a customer — the message is refused naming the empty field.
</Rule>

Merge fields a template can use: customer, contact, city, phone, outstanding, last order date and value, the oldest bill's number and due date, **every** stated open bill as a list, today's date, the latest dated promise, and the owner.

<Callout kind="money">
Outstanding and the bills list in a message include **stated** bills only. A balance nobody has stated is not something to compose a message about.
</Callout>

## Copied is not sent

<Rule title="Only a confirmed send counts">
A manual message moves **prepared → copied → confirmed sent**. Only confirming it sets the customer's *last confirmed WhatsApp* date, which is what holds them back from the Call Log for <Setting k="queue.whatsappCooldownDays" /> days; only confirming a payment reminder records a collections attempt on the WhatsApp channel. Copying touches none of it.
</Rule>

<Callout kind="why">
A copied-but-unconfirmed message is a customer who may or may not have been contacted. Treating it as sent would hold somebody back from a call on the strength of a message that never left the telecaller's clipboard; treating it as not sent would ring a customer who heard from us an hour ago. It is shown as exactly what it is.
</Callout>

### Copies nobody confirmed

Every hour, copies older than <Setting k="whatsapp.unconfirmedExpiryHours" /> hours are counted for the manager. If <Setting k="whatsapp.autoConfirmAfterHours" /> is above zero, copies older than that are confirmed automatically; at zero nothing ever is, because asserting that a message was sent when the system cannot know is exactly the failure the confirm step exists to prevent.

### Automatic sends

Wati reports *sent*, *delivered* and *read*. Its updates are applied in order and only ever move forward, so a retried or late notification changes nothing. A message Wati accepts counts as sent for the Call Log and for collections, just as a confirmed paste does.

<Rule title="A weekly limit on automatic messages">
No customer receives more than <Setting k="whatsapp.contactsPerWeekLimit" /> messages a week through the API (zero means no limit). A person pasting by hand is exercising judgement; a button that sends is not.
</Rule>

## Customers reached two ways

<Rule title="Both is an instruction about a customer, never a message">
A message goes to exactly one place. A customer set to **both** produces **two** messages — personal first, because it can complete without a person, then the group. Each has its own state; one idempotency key covers the pair, so a retried click cannot double-send either half.
</Rule>

- One confirmed leg **does** set the last-WhatsApp date — waiting for the second would chase somebody who has already heard from us.
- Neither leg may borrow the other's confirmation: the group can be confirmed while the personal one is still copied, and both are shown that way.
- **Both** with no group recorded makes only the personal message.

## Payment reminders and collections

A payment reminder is a template whose purpose is *payment reminder*, with a stage it belongs to. When one is confirmed sent (or accepted by Wati) for a customer on the collections worklist, a WhatsApp **attempt** is written, keyed on the message, and the collections cadence dates the next reminder from it. See [Payment Follow-up](/docs/crm/payments/how-it-works).

<Callout kind="why">
No path that sent a reminder used to write that attempt, so a stage 1 customer came back due a message every single day.
</Callout>

## Replies

Inbound messages arrive from Wati and are matched to a customer by the last ten digits of the number. A reply is **to action** until somebody replies or marks it handled. A number that WhatsApp says cannot receive messages is listed as undeliverable, and automatic rules stop trying it rather than reporting "sending failing".

## Where the code and the intent disagree today

- **"WhatsApp mode" changes nothing.** The setting is on the Settings screen, but whether a message goes automatically is decided by the founder's switch and whether a key exists — the setting is never read.
- **Copy, confirm and cancel do not check the message is in your book.** They act on whatever message id they are given, for anybody who is not narrowed to read-only.
- **Automatic confirmation of old copies would fail if switched on.** The hourly job calls the same confirm step a person uses, and that step requires a signed-in user to record who confirmed — a scheduled job has none.
- **The weekly limit is counted over the last seven days in the database's own clock**, not the business week.
