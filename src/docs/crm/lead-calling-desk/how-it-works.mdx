The Calling desk is where **leads nobody will visit** — website, WhatsApp, portal and marketplace enquiries — are qualified by telephone. It sits on top of the lead ladder rather than beside it: the ladders, the gates and the sales manager's verification all work exactly as described on [All Leads](/docs/crm/leads/how-it-works). What the desk adds is one rule about **how many calls a lead gets** and one rule about **when it is ready to become a Prospect**.

<Callout kind="live">
Settings quoted here are read live from your CRM.
</Callout>

## The three-call rule

A lead on the desk gets **at most three qualification calls** — Call 1, Call 2, Call 3. There is no fourth. The calls exist to collect the answers a Prospect needs; the moment those are in, calling stops, whichever call it was.

<Flow caption="What one call does to a lead" chart={`
flowchart TD
  C["A qualification call is saved"] --> O{"Outcome"}
  O -- "Not interested" --> L1["Lost<br/>(Customer not interested)"]
  O -- "Wrong or invalid number" --> L2["Lost<br/>(Wrong lead)"]
  O -- "Spoke / call back / no answer" --> R{"Competitor, product and<br/>monthly litres all on record?"}
  R -- "Yes" --> RF["Ready for Prospect<br/>(no further call offered)"]
  R -- "No" --> N{"Was that Call 3?"}
  N -- "No" --> NX["Next call owed"]
  N -- "Yes" --> CP{"Competitor captured?"}
  CP -- "Yes" --> EX["Three calls used<br/>(still a Suspect — finish on the record or close)"]
  CP -- "No, and they never answered" --> L3["Lost<br/>(No response after 3 attempts)"]
  CP -- "No, but they did speak" --> L4["Lost<br/>(Required information not obtained)"]
`} />

<Rule title="Every outcome uses a call">
Spoke, asked to call back, no answer, wrong number and not interested all count against the three. A **message** sent to the lead (brochure, price list, WhatsApp follow-up) uses no call.
</Rule>

<Rule title="Only the competitor is compulsory">
Of the eleven answers the desk can collect, only **which brand they use now** closes the lead if it is still missing after Call 3. **Product** and **monthly requirement** are needed for a Prospect but are never a reason to close: a blank there is "not yet captured", and the lead stays a Suspect marked *Three calls used*.
</Rule>

<Callout kind="why">
"Never once answered" and "answered but would not say" are different losses, and the desk records them as different reasons so they can be counted later. A competitor is the one answer without which a Prospect is not worth a sales manager's call; a missing product or litre figure is something somebody can still fill in on the record.
</Callout>

## No question is asked twice

What is still owed is whatever is still **empty**. An answer captured on Call 1 is a filled field on the record, so it is not offered on Call 2 — not because a rule hides it, but because there is nothing left to ask. Each answer has a **suggested** call (competitor, product, litres, customer type and application on Call 1; decision maker and GST on Call 2; buyer, credit days, address and email on Call 3). On Call 3 every answer a Prospect still needs is asked regardless.

Nothing about "ready" or "which call is next" is stored. *Ready for Prospect* is simply the answer to "are those answers on the record", and *which call* is a count of the qualification calls already made. A stored flag would be a second copy free to drift the day somebody types a competitor in somewhere else.

## Becoming a Prospect

When a lead is ready, the telecaller presses **Convert to Prospect**, chooses a coded reason from <Setting k="leads.prospectReasons" />, and fills anything the Prospect gate still needs (sales type, kind of business, contact person). What happens next depends on one switch:

<Setting k="leads.callingDeskDirectPromotion" />

| Switch | What Convert to Prospect does |
|---|---|
| **On** | The lead becomes a **Prospect** at once, through the same gate as any other move, and the sales manager is given a verification next action due <Setting k="leads.verificationDueDays" /> days out. Message: *Prospect created. Waiting for Sales Manager verification.* |
| **Off** | The lead stays a **Suspect** and is put forward as a **request** (*Awaiting verification*). The sales manager's verification call is what promotes it — due on the next working day. Message: *Prospect requested.* |

<Callout kind="why">
Both paths read the same readiness test and the same Prospect gate, so a lead accepted by one can never be refused by the other for something it was never asked. The switch decides only **who is trusted to press the button that promotes**. Turning it off sends everything back through the same verification queue with no second code path kept alive.
</Callout>

### The request states

A request is not a rung: the lead stays a Suspect until the sales manager acts. It is held beside the stage as one of three states.

| State on the desk | Meaning | What the desk can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Awaiting verification** | With the sales manager | Nothing — no call is offered, and the lead cannot be closed |
| **Manager follow-up** | The manager is holding it while they follow up | Nothing until they decide |
| **Returned by Manager** | Sent back with a note | Read the note; resubmit or mark Lost |

When the sales manager records a **verified** verification, the request settles: the lead moves to Prospect through the ordinary gated move under the manager's own permission, and the manager takes the lead-manager seat. *Not qualified* closes the lead as lost through the verification's own closure.

### Who verifies

The verifier is found in this order, and the telecaller chooses nobody:

1. If a **sales manager raised the lead herself**, the person named in the *self-raised verifier* setting (`leads.selfRaisedVerifierEmail`) — nobody verifies their own work.
2. If a calling-desk worker owns the lead, the person named in the *telecaller verifier* setting (`leads.telecallerVerifierEmail`) — strictly: a name that matches nobody who can verify refuses the conversion with a message rather than routing it elsewhere.
3. The lead's sales manager seat, if filled.
4. The lead manager seat, if filled.
5. Otherwise the first eligible candidate from the org chart. If there is none, the conversion is refused and says so.

## The work queue

*Today's work queue* holds three kinds of lead, in this order: **Ready for Prospect**, **Returned**, then **Due** (a call or message due today or earlier, soonest first; a brand-new lead nobody scheduled counts as *Call 1 due today*). Leads with the sales manager, or with a next step on a later day, are not on it.

The figures across the top filter the same list without navigating, and every filter is in the address bar.

## Closing and reopening

- **Mark Lost** takes one of the desk's six reasons (*No response after 3 attempts*, *Required information not obtained*, *Customer not interested*, *Requirement not genuine*, *Wrong lead — should not have been raised*, *Other*). Each is filed under a code from <Setting k="leads.lostReasons" />. A lead cannot be closed while the sales manager has it.
- **Reverse Lead** reopens a lost lead with a coded reason from <Setting k="leads.reopenReasons" />. It returns to the rung it was lost from; if that was Suspect, **the three calls start again**. Earlier calls stay on the record. A lead closed because verification failed can only be reversed by the sales manager.
- **Next action** can only be changed while the lead is a Suspect.

## Who can use the desk, and whose leads they see

<Rule title="The desk is granted per person">
The Calling desk is its own module, off by default even on a whole-CRM grant. Every write the desk makes checks the module itself — not only the screen — because the underlying lead permission is held by every associate and the route guard alone would be a courtesy, not a door.
</Rule>

The desk narrows the way every other CRM list does: a telecaller sees **their own book**, a manager **their reports'** books. It deliberately does not use the territory narrowing the All Leads list uses, which answers "everybody" for anyone without a territory.

**Leads with no owner** are on nobody's desk. Somebody whose view is wider than one book sees a banner counting them and can assign each to a person who holds the desk; people who cannot open the desk are listed greyed out with the reason. Assigning notifies whoever can hand leads out.

## Where the code and the intent disagree today

- **"Five answers" versus three.** The setting description for direct promotion and the engine's header both say "the five required answers"; the code's readiness test (`prospectProgress`) needs three — competitor, product, monthly litres (`engines/lead-calling-desk.ts` `DESK_FIELDS`).
- **Two verification due dates.** Direct promotion dates the sales manager's verification <Setting k="leads.verificationDueDays" /> days out; a request is dated the next working day (`actions/lead-calling-desk.ts` `convertLeadToProspect` vs `requestProspect`).
