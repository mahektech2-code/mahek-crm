A purchase order is the point where the business commits to paying a vendor. Everything about it follows from three rules: **no purchase without a PO**, **a PO is made the way the item's purchase rule says, and is not approved**, and **what has been received against it is read from the gate, never typed**.

## Making a PO

<Rule title="The item's purchase rule decides how the PO is made">
A **Quotation** item is bought on the Quotation manager: **Purchase it** writes the PO there and then, already **Sent**, and a requirement can also **Link PO** to a number the Quotation manager made. A **Direct purchase** item gets its PO from **Create PO** on the requirement, **Approved** and ready to send. A **Buyer decision** item may do either. See [Purchase requirements](/docs/erp/purchase-requirements/how-it-works).
</Rule>

<Rule title="No PO is approved">
Every route writes the PO ready to send and to receive against. Nobody approves it, and nothing waits on an approver.
</Rule>

<Callout kind="why">
The checks that stand before a PO now sit earlier: the requirement's blind count and its comparison with the book, and, for a quotation item, the Quotation manager, where only a rate a manager has marked **Ready to buy** can be bought by anybody who is not a manager. Older POs still at Pending approval finish on the old path, below.
</Callout>

<Rule title="A PO is built from requirements, never typed">
Every PO line answers a requirement for that PO's vendor, and a requirement may be on only one live PO. There is no free-typed item or supplier. A Quotation manager purchase with no requirement waiting raises one for itself, so the rule holds there too.
</Rule>

<Rule title="Where the rate comes from">
On **Create PO** the rate is the item's price in Masters → Raw materials, never read from the form; an item with no price is ordered at ₹0. On a Quotation manager PO it is the rate in the bought cell. On **New PO** and **Edit** on this screen the rate and GST are typed per line, and a rate must be above zero.
</Rule>

<Rule title="Typing a rate needs money">
**New PO** and **Edit** on this screen need the **View purchase money** power, because the rates are typed there. **Create PO** on a requirement needs the Purchase orders screen but not the power — the rate is the item's price, and without the power it is used without being shown. **Link PO** needs neither.
</Rule>

Create PO and a Quotation manager PO take GST at <Setting k="erp.purchase.poGstPercent" />%; New PO starts each line at eighteen per cent, changeable. The form fills itself from what is known: delivery date from the requirement's required-by date, payment terms from the vendor's credit days. The delivery date may not be before the PO date. The value is the sum of each line's quantity × rate plus its GST, plus freight once for the whole PO.

**New PO** offers only requirements that are ready for a PO and already carry a vendor: a direct-purchase item whose master names a default vendor is given it when the requirement is raised, and a requirement whose PO was cancelled keeps its vendor. **Edit** is offered on an Approved PO with nothing received, until it is shared.

## POs raised before approval was dropped

A PO still at **Pending approval** keeps its old path until it is decided. **Approve** and **Send back** need the **Approve purchase orders** power, given in Admin Console → Access and nowhere else, and whoever raised the PO cannot approve it unless they are an ERP administrator. Approving moves its requirements to Order Placed; sending back marks the PO Rejected and returns its requirements to waiting for a PO, with their vendor. Such a PO is not shared, printed or received against until it is approved.

## Sharing

<Rule title="Share is offered once a PO is ready">
Share is offered on Approved, Sent, Partly received and Received POs. Only a PO still at Pending approval from before is refused: "A PO is approved before it goes to anybody."
</Rule>

<Rule title="The PDF carries the rates, so sharing needs purchase money">
The **View purchase money** power, the same one the printed PO needs. Without it the Share button is drawn disabled and says why.
</Rule>

A share goes one of three ways:

| Way | Where it can be sent for you | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp | Through Wati, when the founder's WhatsApp switch is on, a key is set, and <Setting k="erp.purchase.poWatiTemplate" /> names an **approved** template asking only for what a PO can fill | Opens on the sender's own WhatsApp with the message written, one button per person |
| Email | From MahekOne with the PO's PDF attached, when a mail provider is set up | Opens in the sender's mail app with the message and the link |
| Copy link | — | The link and the message, to paste anywhere |

Every message carries the PO's link: a PDF of the PO that opens **without signing in**, so a vendor can open it from a phone. The link is made the first time the PO is shared and stays the same after that. It stops answering when the PO is cancelled, sent back or closed.

Each person a PO goes to is recorded — how, the number or email, the message, and what became of it (sent, sent from the person's own app, failed, copied; Wati's delivered and read ticks follow). The first share moves an Approved PO to **Sent**; the list's **Last shared** column is the newest share. A person typed in is checked before anything goes: a number WhatsApp cannot reach or an address that is not an email is refused by name, never dropped. One share reaches at most ten people.

## Receiving

<Rule title="Only an open PO is received against">
Goods inward names an Approved, Sent or Partly received PO and one of its lines. Nothing is received against a PO that is received in full, closed, cancelled, or still awaiting approval from before. See [Goods receipt](/docs/erp/goods-receipt-and-testing/how-it-works).
</Rule>

<Rule title="What has been received is read, never stored">
A PO line's received quantity is the sum of the inward lines and hand-entered register lots against it, read each time. After every receipt the PO's status and its requirements' status are rewritten from that figure in the same save: some received is Partly received, every line in full is Received.
</Rule>

<Rule title="A little over is fine; a lot over needs its own PO">
A line may receive up to <Setting k="erp.purchase.receiptTolerancePercent" />% more than was ordered — drums never weigh exactly what was ordered. Beyond that the receipt is refused: "Raise a requirement for the extra."
</Rule>

## Ending a PO early

| | Close short | Cancel |
|---|---|---|
| When | Partly received | Approved or Sent (or a leftover Pending approval), nothing received |
| Its requirements | Count as Received | Return to waiting for a PO, with their vendor |
| Reason | Required | Required |

<Callout kind="why">
A PO with goods against it has become a purchase. Cancelling it would make goods in the godown belong to nothing, so it is closed short instead and the reason says why the rest never came.
</Callout>

A Quotation manager PO is also taken back by **Unlink** on its requirement's record, or by undoing the purchase on the Quotation manager — both refused once goods have been received.

## On the dashboard

POs to send to the vendor (Approved, not shared yet), POs awaiting delivery, deliveries overdue, and — with purchase money — the open PO value. A "POs awaiting approval" tile shows only while a PO raised before approval was dropped is still waiting. The sidebar badge counts Approved POs, plus those leftover Pending approval POs for somebody holding the approve power.

## Where the code and the intent disagree today

- A WhatsApp or email opened on the sender's own phone counts as shared, and moves the PO to Sent, before the person has actually pressed send in their app.
- WhatsApp through Wati carries the PO's link, not the PDF itself: the template has no document header.
- **Close short** and **Cancel PO** need only the screen — no power — although each changes what its requirements say.
- The company name on the PDF and the message is fixed in the code.
- The screen's own subtitle still reads "Raised from requirements, approved, sent and received against", and the **Approve purchase orders** power is still described as approving a PO "before it can go to the vendor" — both from before approval was dropped.
- **New PO** and **Edit** let a rate be typed, so a direct-purchase line's rate can be changed away from the item's price before the PO is shared.
