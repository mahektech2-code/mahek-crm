A purchase order is the point where a decision about money is made by somebody other than the person who wants the goods. Everything about it follows from three rules: **no purchase without a PO**, **a PO is raised for approval and approved by somebody else**, and **what has been received against it is read from the gate, never typed**.

## Raising

<Rule title="A PO is built from requirements, never typed">
A PO line is a requirement that is Ready for PO, for this PO's vendor, not already on another live PO. There is no free-typed item or supplier: a PO can only buy what a department asked for, from the vendor that was chosen for it.
</Rule>

<Rule title="The rate of a quotation item is the quotation's">
A requirement bought by quotation must be ordered at the selected quotation's rate. Negotiation is recorded on the quotation, where it can be compared, rather than hidden on the PO.
</Rule>

<Rule title="Money is needed to raise one">
Raising or editing a PO needs the **purchase money** power, because it sets rates.
</Rule>

The form fills itself from what is known: delivery date from the quotation's delivery days (or the requirement's required-by date), payment terms from the quotation (or the vendor's credit days), freight from the quotation, GST at eighteen per cent unless changed. The delivery date may not be before the PO date. The value is the sum of each line's quantity × rate plus its GST, plus freight once for the whole PO.

## Approving

<Rule title="Raised for approval, approved by somebody else">
Approve and Send back need the **Approve purchase orders** power, given in Admin Console → Access and nowhere else. Whoever raised a PO cannot approve it unless they are an ERP administrator.
</Rule>

<Callout kind="why">
The person who wants the goods should not be the person who commits the business to paying for them. Making the PO a separate, approved record is what gives every purchase a second pair of eyes — and a name against the decision in the audit log.
</Callout>

<Rule title="Every line must be approvable by you">
A PO carries several materials. Where an ERP administrator has named who approves a material, a PO carrying it can be decided only by somebody named for **every** line; where no approver is named, the power decides as before.
</Rule>

Approving moves the PO's requirements to **Order Placed**. Sending back marks the PO Rejected and returns its requirements to Ready for PO with their vendor, so they can be raised again with the correction.

## Sharing

<Rule title="Only an approved PO goes to anybody">
Share is offered on Approved, Sent, Partly received and Received POs. A Pending approval PO is refused: "A PO is approved before it goes to anybody."
</Rule>

<Rule title="The PDF carries the rates, so sharing needs purchase money">
The **View purchase money** power, the same one the printed PO needs. Without it the Share button is drawn disabled and says why.
</Rule>

A share goes one of three ways:

| Way | Where it can be sent for you | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp | Through Wati, when the founder's WhatsApp switch is on, a key is set, and <Setting k="erp.purchase.poWatiTemplate" /> names an **approved** template asking only for what a PO can fill | Opens on the sender's own WhatsApp with the message written, one button per person |
| Email | From MahekOne with the PO's PDF attached, when a mail provider is set up | Opens in the sender's mail app with the message and the link |
| Copy link | — | The link and the message, to paste anywhere |

Every message carries the PO's link: a PDF of the PO that opens **without signing in**, so a vendor can open it from a phone. The link is made the first time the PO is shared and stays the same after that. It stops answering when the PO is cancelled, sent back or closed.

Each person a PO goes to is recorded — how, the number or email, the message, and what became of it (sent, sent from the person's own app, failed, copied; Wati's delivered and read ticks follow). The first share moves an Approved PO to **Sent**; the list's **Last shared** column is the newest share. A person typed in is checked before anything goes: a number WhatsApp cannot reach or an address that is not an email is refused by name, never dropped. One share reaches at most ten people.

## Receiving

<Rule title="Only an approved PO is received against">
Goods inward names an approved, sent or partly received PO and one of its lines. Nothing is received against a PO awaiting approval, closed, cancelled or rejected. See [Goods receipt](/docs/erp/goods-receipt-and-testing/how-it-works).
</Rule>

<Rule title="What has been received is read, never stored">
A PO line's received quantity is the sum of the inward lines and hand-entered register lots against it, read each time. After every receipt the PO's status and its requirements' status are rewritten from that figure in the same save: some received is Partly received, every line in full is Received.
</Rule>

<Rule title="A little over is fine; a lot over needs its own PO">
A line may receive up to <Setting k="erp.purchase.receiptTolerancePercent" />% more than was ordered — drums never weigh exactly what was ordered. Beyond that the receipt is refused: "Raise a requirement for the extra."
</Rule>

## Ending a PO early

| | Close short | Cancel |
|---|---|---|
| When | Partly received | Pending approval, Approved or Sent, nothing received |
| Its requirements | Count as Received | Return to Ready for PO, with their vendor |
| Reason | Required | Required |

<Callout kind="why">
A PO with goods against it has become a purchase. Cancelling it would make goods in the godown belong to nothing, so it is closed short instead and the reason says why the rest never came.
</Callout>

## On the dashboard

POs awaiting approval ("you approve these" for an approver), POs to send to the vendor, POs awaiting delivery, deliveries overdue, and — with purchase money — the open PO value (approved, not yet fully received). The sidebar badge counts POs waiting on approval or on being sent.

## Where the code and the intent disagree today

- A WhatsApp or email opened on the sender's own phone counts as shared, and moves the PO to Sent, before the person has actually pressed send in their app.
- WhatsApp through Wati carries the PO's link, not the PDF itself: the template has no document header.
- **Close short** and **Cancel PO** need only the screen — no power — although each changes what its requirements say.
- The company name on the PDF and the message is fixed in the code.
