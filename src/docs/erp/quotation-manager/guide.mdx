The **Quotation manager** is the purchase desk's table of supplier rates, and it works like a spreadsheet. Each **row** is one quotation — the date and the party (supplier) who quoted — and each **column** is something quoted for: the pack on top, the material in the middle and the brand underneath, exactly as the desk heads them. A column can also hold text, such as the broker's name.

Everything on it can be changed in place: the cells, the date and party of a row, and the columns themselves. The sidebar starts shut on this screen to give the table room; the button beside the app switcher opens it.

## Working the table

- **Click a cell and type** to replace it; **double-click** or press **Enter** to change what is there.
- **Enter** saves and moves down, **Tab** saves and moves right, **Esc** cancels.
- **Arrow keys** walk the table; hold **Shift** (or drag) to select a block.
- **Delete** clears the selected cells.
- **Ctrl+C** copies the selection and **Ctrl+V** pastes a block copied from Excel or Google Sheets, starting at the selected cell. Rows past the bottom are added. A value that is not a number in a rate column is left out and the screen says which.
- **Ctrl+Z** (or **Undo**) takes back the last change; **Ctrl+Y** (or **Redo**) puts it back.

Every change is saved as you make it. The words beside the period say whether everything is saved.

The **editing bar** above the table says what the selected cell is — the material, the party, the date, what the rate is per (the raw material's own unit) and the rate. The **footer** adds up the selection: how many rates, the lowest and the average.

**Sort** shows the rows latest first (as they are kept), oldest first, by party, or cheapest first in one column. **Freeze** keeps nothing, the date and party, or everything up to the selected column in view while you scroll sideways. Both are only how you look at the table.

## Choosing a party or a broker

The **Party** column, and any text column such as **Broker name**, open a menu rather than a typing box. It shows ten at a time with the current one ticked. With more than ten to choose from it has a search box, and **Show more** brings the next ten. A name that isn't on the list is added from the same box (**Add “…”**), and **Clear** empties the cell.

## Buying a rate

1. **Ready to buy (green edge).** A manager or administrator selects the rate to buy (one cell or a block) and chooses **Mark → Ready to buy**. The cell gets a green edge: the team may now buy it.
2. **Purchase it.** Anybody on the team selects a green-edged cell and presses **Purchase it**, right-clicks it, or presses **Purchase it at ₹…** on a requirement in the side panel. The dialog shows the rate (and whether it is the cheapest party's), the quote date and the pack. They pick the **requirement** it is for (the one waiting is picked for them, or *None* to raise one with the purchase), enter the **number of drums** (where the pack's net is in the material's unit the dialog says what the drums come to), check the **quantity**, the godown it is **delivered to** and the **delivery date**, read what the **PO comes to** with GST, and press **Purchase it**. Where the party already has an open PO from here, the **PO number** box offers **Add to PO-…** beside **New PO number**, the one made today chosen — several materials bought from one party together are one PO, a line each. Otherwise MahekOne makes the **PO number** for them (PO-1243, the next number in the ERP's PO series) and, with it, the **purchase order itself**, under Purchase → Purchase orders: sent, at this rate, ready for goods to be received against it. Nobody types the number and nobody has to link it.
3. **Bought (orange edge).** The cell gets an orange edge with a small mark in its corner, like a note in Excel. **Hover** over it to read the purchase: the PO number, drums, rate, material, party, quote date, and who bought it and when. **Click** it to pin the note, then **Edit** or **Undo purchase**, which puts it back to Ready to buy.

Only a manager or an administrator marks a rate Ready to buy or takes the mark off. A rate that isn't Ready to buy can't be bought, except by a manager.

**Right-click** any cell for everything it offers, in three groups: what to look at (the PO note, compare this material in the side panel, copy), what to change (Purchase it, edit, the marks, add or duplicate a row), and what to take back (undo purchase, delete the row) in red at the end. Only what your access lets you do is listed: changing anything needs **Edit** on the Quotation manager, and taking something back needs **Delete**.

## The side panel

The panel beside the table reads the selected rate: the material, the party and the date, the rate in large type with **cheapest this month** where it is, every party's **latest** rate for the same material (cheapest first — click one to go to it), this party's last few quotes as small bars, and every **requirement waiting** for the material with **Purchase it at ₹…**, which opens Purchase it with that requirement picked. The panel button at the right of the toolbar hides it.

A column heading says **REQ-n waiting** when a requirement is waiting for the raw material the column is linked to.

**PO / order cancelled** (red edge) and **Material picked up by transporter** (blue edge) can be put on by anybody, except over a Ready to buy or bought rate, which only a manager changes. Marking a bought rate cancelled takes its line off the purchase order, as Undo purchase does; marking it picked up keeps the PO.

## Rows

Rows are listed **latest first**: the newest date on top, and within a date the row entered last first.

Use the **⋯** beside a row number, or right-click any cell of the row, to add a row with the same date, duplicate the row, or delete it. A date can be typed as `14-Oct-2026`, `14/10/2026` or `2026-10-14`. The party offers the purchase parties as you type, and a name that matches one exactly is linked to it.

## Columns

**+ Material** adds a column: the raw material it is for (what Purchase it puts on the PO), its three heading lines, whether it holds rates or text, and where it goes — with the heading drawn as it will look. Double-click a heading, right-click it, or use its **⋯** to edit it, insert another after it, move it left or right, freeze up to it, sort by it, or remove it. **Remove column** hides it; its rates are kept, and **Removed columns** (in the footer) brings it back.

## The cheapest rate

The cheapest rate in each column, among the rows shown, is drawn in **green**. Cancelled rates do not count, and a column with a single rate has no cheapest.
