The Quotation manager replaces the desk's own rate sheet, so it keeps the sheet's shape and its colours rather than imposing a form on it.

## Rows and columns are both data

Neither the materials nor the broker column are written into the code. A column is a row in `erp_quote_columns` with three heading lines, a kind (rates or text) and a position, so the desk adds, renames, moves and removes them from the screen. The columns the screen first opened with were the desk's own sheet's.

## Nothing is thrown away

Removing a column archives it, and deleting a row marks it deleted. The rates under either are kept, so a column brought back comes back with every rate it held.

## A change is saved as it is made, and all or nothing

Every edit — one cell, a paste of hundreds, a colour across a block — is sent as one list of changes and written in one transaction, so a paste is never half saved. The screen shows the change at once; if it is refused, the screen takes it back and says why.

## What a cell may hold

A rate column holds a number, or nothing. Commas, spaces and a rupee sign are taken out of what is pasted; anything else is refused rather than stored as a rate nobody can compare. A text column holds up to 200 characters.

## Buying: grey, then yellow

A rate is chosen by a manager and bought by the team, in that order:

- **Ready to buy (grey)** is put on, or taken off, only by an ERP manager or administrator. Only a rate with a figure in it can be Ready to buy.
- **Purchase it** turns a grey rate yellow and records the **number of drums**, which may not be left out. The **PO number is made by the server** at that moment, the next number of the ERP's own PO series, so nobody can mistype one — unless the dialog adds the purchase to an open PO of the same supplier's made here (Approved, Sent or Partly received, not yet carrying that material), which it offers and defaults to when one was made today: then the purchase takes that number and the PO gains a line. Editing a purchase keeps its number; undoing it gives the number up, and buying the rate again makes a new one. A rate that isn't grey can't be bought, except by a manager.
- **The purchase writes its purchase order.** In the same save the server writes an ERP purchase order for that PO number: the row's supplier, the column's raw material, the quoted rate, the quantity, godown and delivery date from the dialog, and the drums. It is `Sent` with no approval step, like a linked one. Its line answers the requirement picked in the dialog, or, with none waiting, a requirement raised with the purchase (marked as raised by the Quotation manager). A second material bought under the same number of the same supplier joins the same PO. Changing the drums changes the line; undoing the purchase takes the line off (cancelling the PO when it is empty) and is refused once goods have been received. A requirement the purchase raised is cancelled with it; one somebody raised goes back to waiting. A row whose party is not a supplier, or a column with no raw material, can't be bought this way.
- **Undo purchase** puts a yellow rate back to grey, and anybody may do it, because it corrects a purchase entered by mistake. Any other change to a bought rate is a manager's.

The screen offers only what is allowed, and the server refuses the rest whatever the screen sent. The PO number, the drums, and who bought it and when are kept on the cell and shown in its note.

## The other marks

**PO / order cancelled** (red) and **Material picked up by transporter** (blue) are put on by anybody — except over a Ready to buy or bought rate, which only a manager changes. A bought rate marked cancelled gives up its PO line exactly as Undo purchase does; one marked picked up keeps it, and a picked-up rate still counts as bought when a requirement links its PO number. A cancelled rate is left out of the latest and lowest rates.

## The period

The screen opens on the last six months. Choose three months, every date, or your own dates; the search narrows by party. At most the newest 3,000 rows of a period are drawn, and the screen says so when that cut anything off.

## Who can use it

Anybody given the **Quotation manager** screen on the ERP. It needs no power; marking or unmarking Ready to buy, and changing a bought rate other than undoing it, need the manager or admin level on the ERP grant. A preview of the ERP as somebody else is read only.

## Where the code and the intent disagree today

The grid scrolls sideways when there are more columns than fit, unlike the ERP's lists — it is a sheet, and folding its columns away would hide rates.
