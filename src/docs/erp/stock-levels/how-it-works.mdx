Levels answer one question for the store: **of everything we keep, what needs buying or making now?** They are deliberately simple — a minimum (and for raw material a maximum) per item per godown — and every figure beside them is worked out from the stock ledger at the moment the screen opens. Nothing on these screens is a stored balance.

## Two kinds of level

| | Raw-material level | Finished-goods level |
|---|---|---|
| Set per | item and godown | SKU and godown |
| Figures | minimum and maximum | minimum only |
| Stock read from | the raw-material lots at that godown (drums: company-wide) | FG stock in cans for a loose SKU; packing stock in boxes for a boxed SKU |
| Shortfall acted on by | a purchase requirement | production |

<Callout kind="why" title="Why a finished-goods level has no maximum">
A raw material is bought, and a purchase needs a quantity — "buy enough to reach the maximum" gives one. A finished good is made in batches whose size is set by the recipe and the packing plan, so a maximum would be a number nobody acts on.
</Callout>

## What counts as available

<Rule title="Available is read from the lots, never typed">
Raw material reads every lot's remaining stock at that godown. Lot stock is already net of the cans filled and the boxes packed, so it is not subtracted again. A boxed SKU reads **packing stock** (boxes); a loose SKU reads **FG stock** (cans) — a boxed SKU is one whose packing record needs empty boxes.
</Rule>

<Rule title="Drums are company-wide">
A **Drum** level reads every drum bought on a purchase less every drum sold on an order, whatever the godown. That is how the source system counted drums, and the ERP kept it.
</Rule>

## The re-order figures

| Figure | Raw material | Finished goods |
|---|---|---|
| **Re-order %** | available ÷ midpoint of min and max, minus one | available ÷ minimum |
| At the level | 0 at the midpoint | 100 at the minimum |
| **Required** | maximum − available, blank when at or above the maximum | — |

A raw-material item with min 100 and max 200 holding 75 reads **-50%** and needs **125**. A finished good with min 60 holding 30 reads **50%**.

## When is something "below"

<Rule title="Finished goods: under the minimum">
A **followed** SKU is short when its minimum is greater than what is available. That is the whole rule, and it is what puts the SKU on Below minimum and raises the alert.
</Rule>

<Rule title="Raw material: under the minimum">
A **followed** raw-material level is listed, flagged and alerted when stock is **below its minimum**. **Required** still says how much brings it up to the maximum, and **Raise requirement** is offered on any row with room under the maximum, so a buyer can top up early without the store being told it is short.
</Rule>

<Callout kind="why" title="Why UnFollow exists">
Some items are worth keeping a level for — a reference figure for the buyer — without wanting the store nagged about them. UnFollow keeps the level and silences every consequence: the tab, the flag, the alert and the notification.
</Callout>

## Raising a requirement

From a raw-material row the **Raise requirement** action opens the ordinary new purchase-requirement form with these answers already in it:

| Field | Filled with |
|---|---|
| Godown | The level's godown |
| Material type and item | The level's |
| Required quantity | **Required** — enough to reach the maximum |
| Priority | For Stock |
| Department | The person's own team where they raise for only one; otherwise the team that asks for that material category |

Everything after that — the purchase rule, quotations, the PO — is the normal purchase flow. See [Purchase requirements](/docs/erp/purchase-requirements).

## Planned levels

<Rule title="Every raw material holds a week and a half, worked out from its own history">
Each night the planner reads every raw material's **production use** (SFG batch lines for chemicals, fills for cans, packing for boxes) and its **purchases** (the purchase register's posted lots) at each godown, as far back as <Setting k="erp.levels.historyYears" /> years, and sets:

- **Minimum** — the forecast use over the next <Setting k="erp.levels.coverDays" /> days
- **Maximum** — the minimum plus <Setting k="erp.levels.orderCycleDays" /> more days of forecast use

An item with less than <Setting k="erp.levels.minHistoryWeeks" /> weeks of either history gets no planned level. Drums are left out: they are counted company-wide, not per godown. The whole planner is switched by <Setting k="erp.levels.autoPlan" />.
</Rule>

The forecast for a day is **base rate × month factor × week-of-month factor**:

| Part | Read from | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| Base rate | Production use where there is enough of it, else purchases. The last <Setting k="erp.levels.recentWeeks" /> weeks lead (60%), the last year steadies it (40%) | What empties the shelf now |
| Month factor | Whichever series reaches back further — usually the purchase register | A busy or slow month of the year |
| Week-of-month factor | Production use only | A heavier first or last week of a month |

Each factor is shrunk towards 1 by how much history stands behind it, so one unusual October cannot make every October look like it. A one-off week (a bulk buy, a backlog cleared) is capped before it reaches the rate.

<Callout kind="why" title="Why the week of the month never comes from purchases">
When a drum is bought is the buyer's calendar, not the shelf's. Two purchases on the 1st and the 21st would otherwise read as a shelf that empties in those weeks and stands still in the others, and the level would swing for no reason.
</Callout>

<Rule title="The AI reviews the plan, inside a fixed limit">
Where <Setting k="erp.ai.levels.enabled" /> is on, an AI model reads each item's monthly history, its factors and the window ahead (festivals, the monsoon, the March year-end) and may move the planned level by at most <Setting k="erp.ai.levels.maxAdjustPercent" />% either way, with one sentence saying why. It looks at each item again every <Setting k="erp.ai.levels.reviewEveryDays" /> days, or sooner when the statistics moved a lot, and stops for the month at <Setting k="erp.ai.levels.monthlyCap" /> reviews. With no key, the cap spent or the switch off, the statistical plan stands.
</Rule>

<Rule title="A level a person set is never changed">
A level the planner created is marked **Planned automatically** and rewritten every night. Editing its minimum or maximum by hand takes it off the planner for good, unless somebody presses **Let the planner keep it**. A level typed by hand keeps its figures; the planner's figures are shown beside it as **Planned min / Planned max** for comparison.
</Rule>

The planner runs nightly, on every deploy (once a day), from **Plan levels now** on the Raw-material levels screen, and by hand with `npm run jobs -- erp-levels-plan --dry-run`.

## Suggested finished-goods levels

<Rule title="A suggestion is arithmetic, and it is never applied by itself">
Average daily dispatch over the look-back, times the cover days, rounded up to whole units:

- **Look-back** — <Setting k="erp.ai.reorder.lookbackDays" /> days
- **Minimum** — <Setting k="erp.ai.reorder.minCoverDays" /> days of cover
- **Maximum** — <Setting k="erp.ai.reorder.maxCoverDays" /> days of cover

The feature is switched by <Setting k="erp.ai.reorder.enabled" />. A person applies a suggestion with a button. Raw-material levels use the planner above instead; this 90-day suggestion is shown for one only where the planner has not read the item yet.
</Rule>

## The hourly alert and who is told

The hourly ERP checks raise one **Stock below re-order level** alert per short level, naming the item, the godown, what is available and the minimum. It is raised once and stays open until the stock recovers, at which point the next run resolves it with *Condition cleared*.

When the alert is **first** raised, a notification goes to every active person who is assigned to that godown, holds the ERP, and can raise purchase requirements (they hold the Purchase requirements screen, or their ERP grant is not narrowed at all). Nobody assigned to the godown means nobody is told — the alert is still on the Alerts screen. See [Alerts and the AI helpers](/docs/erp/alerts-and-ai).

## Who can do what

| Act | Needs |
|---|---|
| Open either screen and its tabs | The screen on the person's ERP grant (Raw-material levels, Finished-goods levels) |
| Add, edit, copy, delete, apply suggestions | The same screen — there is no separate power |
| Raise a requisition from a row | The levels screen to open the form; the save is checked against Purchase requirements |
| Be notified of a new shortfall | Assigned to the godown, and able to raise requirements |

Every change is written to the audit log with the before and after values.

## Where the code and the intent disagree today

- **Finished-goods levels are not planned yet.** Only raw materials get the planner; a finished-goods level is still typed by hand or set from its 90-day suggestion.
- **Drum and stationery levels never get a suggestion**, though the bulk **Apply suggestions** button is offered on them and answers *No suggestion to apply*.
- **Drums ignore the godown.** A drum level at two godowns shows the same Available at both.
