**Stock accuracy** is where the ERP checks that what is on the shelf is what the book says. It is the screen behind **Inventory Accuracy Control**: daily cycle counts, counts somebody asks for, and quick stock checks from the floor, each counted without seeing the book. A count that disagrees with the book raises a **red flag** and notifies stock control, and the recount, investigation and any adjustment follow. Stock is never changed quietly — only through an adjustment somebody else approves. **It is not a step of buying**: a purchase requirement opens no count and waits on none.

## Stock checks

A stock check is a quick count of **any** item — a raw material, a semi-finished product, filled cans or packed boxes — made whenever somebody wants to check a shelf. Most come from the Mahek Factory phone, where each department checks the stock it handles; **Record a stock check** enters one here.

<Steps>
<Step title="Record a stock check">
Pick the **Kind of stock**, the **Item** and the **Godown**, and type what you **Counted** — zero if there is none. Add **Where** (bay, rack or bin) and, if you like, why it might differ.
</Step>
<Step title="Save check">
Only now is the book read and compared, by the same rule a count uses: "Matches the book: counted 480, book 482 Ltr", or "Mismatch: counted 450, book 500 Ltr (−50)".
</Step>
</Steps>

A check moves no stock. A mismatch waits on this tab, and on the dashboard as **Stock check mismatches**, until a manager or somebody with the stock-control power presses **Mark reviewed** with what they found. On a raw material they can instead **Open a formal count**: a blind count of that shelf on **To count**, with the red flag, recount and approved adjustment that come with it.

## Counting

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the count and press Count it">
Go to the shelf. Count every drum, can or box of the item, in litres or pieces as the prompt says.
</Step>
<Step title="Enter what you counted">
Write the figure — zero if the shelf is empty — where on the floor you counted, and any lot numbers you saw. A recount is counted in the same place.
</Step>
<Step title="Submit">
Only now is the book shown: "Counted 480 Ltr · book 500. Red flag: −20 Ltr, beyond the 10 tolerance — stock control has been told." A count within tolerance says "Matches the book".
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="note">
A recount is always counted by **somebody other than whoever counted first**. Its **Count it** button is drawn disabled for the first counter, and the server refuses it too.
</Callout>

## A red flag

A red flag sends a notification to everybody with the stock-control power, and every ERP administrator (and, for a count an older Factory phone sent with a requirement, whoever raised it). The person who counted is not notified, because they saw the result on their screen. The notification names the item, the godown, the count, the book and the difference, and opens the red flag. If an independent recount confirms the difference, everyone is notified a second time.

Open the row on **Red flags**. Its record shows the count against the book, every count of it in order, and the **possible causes** the records suggest — goods at the gate not yet in stock, a lot under test, a batch issue, a write-off — each with a link to the record.

<Steps>
<Step title="Ask for a recount">
Opens an independent recount on **To count**. Its result decides what happens next: matching the book resolves the check ("the first count was wrong"); agreeing with the first count confirms the difference and moves the check to **Investigating**; agreeing with neither sends it back for another recount.
</Step>
<Step title="Suggest causes (AI)">
The discrepancy assistant ranks the records the rules found and explains, in a sentence each, whether a **record** looks missing (the goods are there, the paperwork is not) or the **goods** themselves look missing. It is a suggestion; nothing is entered for you.
</Step>
<Step title="Resolve it">
Either enter the missing record on its own screen and press **The missing record is entered** — the book is read again and must now agree with the count — or **Request an adjustment** with a reason. Resolving needs the stock-control power.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Adjustments

An adjustment changes the book to what was counted. It is requested with a reason (damaged, spillage, evaporation, theft or loss, issued or received and never recorded, a measuring difference, other) and a sentence saying what happened, and it posts **only when somebody else approves it**:

| Adjustment | Posts as |
|---|---|
| A shortage | Transfers to **Item Lost Record**, oldest lot first — refused if the lots do not hold it, because stock never goes below zero |
| A surplus | A stock entry on the newest lot, shown in the raw-material entry log as **Stock adjustment** |

Rejecting sends the check back to **Investigating**.

## Purchase requirements are not counted

A purchase requirement used to open a blind count of its item and wait at **Blind count** until it was entered. It no longer does: a requirement goes straight to its purchase method, and its record and the requirements list carry no stock comparison or stock flags. Counts that were open for requirements when this changed were cancelled. To check a shelf before buying, **Ask for a count** here, or make a stock check from the Factory phone.

## On the dashboard

The **Inventory Accuracy Control** strip at the top of the dashboard shows counts to do, red flags, recounts, investigations and adjustments to approve. Every number opens the records behind it. The **Inventory accuracy** section adds stock check mismatches, missing records, how often counts are right first time, the most frequent cause of adjustments, and when the next full count is due.
