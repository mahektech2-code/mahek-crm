**Stock accuracy** is where the ERP checks that what is on the shelf is what the book says. It is the screen behind **Inventory Accuracy Control**: when a purchase requirement is raised, somebody counts the item on the shelf without seeing the book, and the requirement moves on to its purchase method as soon as that count is entered. **Whatever the count finds, the purchase is never stopped.** A count that disagrees with the book raises a **red flag** on the requirement and notifies stock control, and the recount, investigation and any adjustment happen beside the purchase, whenever somebody gets to them. Stock is never changed quietly — only through an adjustment somebody else approves.

| Tab | What it lists |
|---|---|
| **To count** | Counts waiting for somebody to count them, oldest due first. A recount is marked **Independent recount**. |
| **Red flags** | Checks whose count differs from the book beyond the item's tolerance, until they are resolved. Nothing on this tab holds a purchase. |
| **Adjustments** | Every adjustment ever requested — waiting, approved or rejected — with its reason. |
| **Missing records** | Batches, fills, receipts and transfers across the ERP that should have a stock entry and do not. |
| **Every count** | The full history, with who counted, the book at the time and how each was resolved. |

## Counting

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the count and press Count it">
Go to the shelf. Count every drum, can or box of the item, in litres or pieces as the prompt says.
</Step>
<Step title="Enter what you counted">
Write the figure — zero if the shelf is empty — where on the floor you counted, and any lot numbers you saw. A recount is counted in the same place.
</Step>
<Step title="Submit">
Only now is the book shown: "Counted 480 Ltr · book 500. Red flag: −20 Ltr, beyond the 10 tolerance — stock control has been told, and any purchase carries on." A count within tolerance says "Matches the book". Either way, a requirement waiting on the count moves on to its purchase method.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="note">
A recount is always counted by **somebody other than whoever counted first**. Its **Count it** button is drawn disabled for the first counter, and the server refuses it too.
</Callout>

## A red flag

A red flag sends a notification to everybody with the stock-control power, every ERP administrator, and whoever raised the requirement the count was for. The person who counted is not notified, because they saw the result on their screen. The notification names the item, the godown, the count, the book and the difference, and opens the red flag. If an independent recount confirms the difference, everyone is notified a second time.

Fixing it is never urgent for the purchase — the requirement has already moved on. Open the row on **Red flags**. Its drawer shows the count against the book, every count of it in order, and the **possible causes** the records suggest — goods at the gate not yet in stock, a lot under test, a batch issue, a write-off — each with a link to the record.

<Steps>
<Step title="Ask for a recount">
Opens an independent recount on **To count**. Its result decides what happens next: matching the book resolves the check ("the first count was wrong"); agreeing with the first count confirms the difference and moves the check to **Investigating**; agreeing with neither sends it back for another recount.
</Step>
<Step title="Suggest causes (AI)">
The discrepancy assistant ranks the records the rules found and explains, in a sentence each, whether a **record** looks missing (the goods are there, the paperwork is not) or the **goods** themselves look missing. It is a suggestion; nothing is entered for you.
</Step>
<Step title="Resolve it">
Either enter the missing record on its own screen and press **The missing record is entered** — the book is read again and must now agree with the count — or **Request an adjustment** with a reason. Resolving needs the stock-control power.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Adjustments

An adjustment changes the book to what was counted. It is requested with a reason (damaged, spillage, evaporation, theft or loss, issued or received and never recorded, a measuring difference, other) and a sentence saying what happened, and it posts **only when somebody else approves it**:

| Adjustment | Posts as |
|---|---|
| A shortage | Transfers to **Item Lost Record**, oldest lot first — refused if the lots do not hold it, because stock never goes below zero |
| A surplus | A stock entry on the newest lot, shown in the raw-material entry log as **Stock adjustment** |

Rejecting sends the check back to **Investigating**.

## On a purchase requirement

A requirement raised while the stock check is switched on sits at **Blind count** in its flow until the count is entered, then moves on to **Purchase method** whatever the count found. Its drawer says what the count found, what is on the shelf and how much the shelf needs to reach its maximum level. A difference shows up as a flag on the requirements list and never stops the purchase.

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Stage **Blind count** | The count has not been entered yet. Press **Count the stock**. Method, vendor, quotations and the PO wait for this, and only this. |
| Flag **Stock difference — red flag** | The count differs from the book. Stock control has been notified; the purchase carries on. **Open the red flag** shows it. |
| Flag **More than the shelf needs** | The count matched, but more is asked for than the shelf needs to reach its maximum level. Nothing to confirm — it is a warning. |

There is no urgent-purchase request any more: once the count is entered nothing holds the requirement, so there is nothing to bypass.

## On the dashboard

The **Inventory Accuracy Control** strip at the top of the dashboard shows requirements waiting for their count, counts to do, requirements being bought, and then the side track — red flags, recounts, investigations and adjustments to approve. Every number opens the records behind it. The **Inventory accuracy** section adds missing records, how often counts are right first time, the most frequent cause of adjustments, and when the next full count is due.
