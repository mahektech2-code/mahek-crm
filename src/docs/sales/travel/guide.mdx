The Travel ledger is the record of every journey your team made in a month. Each row is one **leg** — a ride from one place to another — with the three ways its distance can be known side by side: what the **odometer** said, what the phone's **GPS track** saw, and anything the salesman **typed by hand**. Beside those is the distance the money was actually paid on, and what that leg is worth.

You open it to answer two questions: *what did the field do this month and what did it cost*, and *is there a leg here I should look at twice*.

<Callout kind="live">
Distances and limits quoted on this page are the settings your deployment is using right now. If somebody changes one in App preferences, this page changes with it.
</Callout>

## Reading a leg

| Column | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Day** | The expense day the leg belongs to. |
| **Salesman** | Who travelled. The name opens his record on [Salesmen](/docs/sales/salesmen). |
| **Mode** | How he travelled — own bike, own car, bus, train, auto and so on. The list of modes is kept by an administrator. |
| **From → to** | Where the leg started and ended, as the handset named them. |
| **Purpose** and **Customer** | Why he went, and which shop, where the leg was tied to a visit. |
| **Odometer** | End reading minus start reading. Only legs on a mode that uses the meter have one. |
| **GPS track** | The phone's own measurement. The track takes a position every few seconds while he is checked in, so it cuts corners and reads **short**. It is the cross-check, not the measurement. |
| **By hand** | A distance he typed, usually for a leg recorded after the journey. |
| **Paid on** | The one distance the policy chose — normally the odometer first, then the track, then the typed figure. |
| **Eligible** | What the leg is worth under the expense policy in force that day. A bus or train leg is worth its ticket, up to the policy's limit. |

<Callout kind="note">
A leg with **no photograph** is not automatically suspicious. A leg typed up on the phone's Travel screen after the journey can never have one — the meter has already moved. The *No photograph* chip only counts legs that claim an odometer distance with no picture of the meter behind it.
</Callout>

## Looking into a leg

<Steps>
<Step title="Start from the banner">
The amber banner counts legs where the odometer and the GPS track disagree by more than 25%. Press the **Disagreeing** chip to list just those.
</Step>
<Step title="Read the GPS coverage first">
"Along the track, 30% covered" means the phone saw little of the journey — a phone in a bag, a dead battery, no signal. A low GPS figure with low coverage tells you about the phone, not the salesman.
</Step>
<Step title="Check whether the reading was added later">
An amber **Added later** pill on the odometer means nobody looked at the meter at the time. Hover it for the reason he gave.
</Step>
<Step title="Open the day">
Press the salesman's name to open his record on [Salesmen](/docs/sales/salesmen), and pick the day. Its **day check** lists every photograph of the day in order — check-in selfies and meter photographs — drawn large enough to read four digits. There you accept or decline each one, and correct a meter reading that was typed wrong. Disagreements also appear on [Flagged expenses](/docs/sales/expenses), with the numbers behind them.
</Step>
</Steps>

## How a journey is recorded on the handset

You will see three kinds of leg. They differ only in when the phone was present:

<Cards cols={3}>
<Card title="A visit journey" tone="brand">Opened when he presses **Start visit** and says how he is travelling, closed when he says he has arrived. On a mode that uses the meter, the phone photographs the meter at **both ends**.</Card>
<Card title="A punch-in session">On an own-vehicle day, the meter is read at the morning punch-in and the evening punch-out, and that pair measures the whole day. The visit journeys inside it are recorded but not paid separately.</Card>
<Card title="A typed-up leg">Entered on the handset's Travel screen once the journey is over. The photograph is optional, because nobody can go back and photograph a meter that has moved.</Card>
</Cards>

At the meter, the phone refuses a reading lower than the one he set off on ("the meter cannot go back") and a trip longer than <Setting k="mbos.travel.maxLegKilometres" /> km — both are nearly always a digit typed wrong.

A journey he calls off is closed **at its own starting reading**, so it measures nothing. It stays on the ledger with a distance of zero rather than disappearing.

## Common questions

<Faq q="A leg shows 'Not yet' under Eligible.">
Nothing has been worked out for that leg yet — usually because no expense policy covers the date, or the day's records are still arriving. Allowances are worked out automatically as each record reaches the office.
</Faq>
<Faq q="A leg has no distance at all.">
There was no odometer pair, no usable GPS track and nothing typed. It is recorded and worth nothing, which is almost never what was meant. Ask the salesman; a meter reading typed wrong can be corrected on his day check.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Why is the GPS figure lower than the odometer almost every time?">
The track joins positions with straight lines, so every bend in the road is cut. That is why the odometer is paid on first and the track is only a cross-check.
</Faq>
<Faq q="A visit journey shows a distance but nothing eligible.">
Either it was on an own-vehicle day (the punch-in and punch-out readings pay for the whole day instead), or it was on a bus or train day (fares are claimed in Expenses), or the meter reading or its photograph was missing. The reason is recorded on the leg ("Not claimed: …").
</Faq>
<Faq q="Can I see the meter photographs here?">
The ledger shows a <b>Photo</b> pill where one exists. The photographs themselves open on the salesman's day check, under his record on the Salesmen screen.
</Faq>
<Faq q="I can see every salesman, not only my team.">
Yes — today the Travel ledger is not narrowed to a manager's own team. Use the month and the salesman link to find the person you want.
</Faq>
