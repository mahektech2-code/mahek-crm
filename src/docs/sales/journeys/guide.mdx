Journeys & visits is where you plan **where each salesman works**, and see **what came of it**. You propose a city for a day; he agrees, or refuses with a reason and sometimes a city he would rather work; then he picks the shops on his phone. Every visit he makes is logged here, with the ones the phone could not verify marked for you to read.

## The four tabs

| Tab | Answers |
|---|---|
| **Team** | The whole team over the last month and the next fortnight — who is planned, who refused, who has nothing. |
| **Today** | Today's plans against today's visits and trails. |
| **One salesman** | One person's month as a calendar or a list, and **Propose days**. |
| **Visit log** | Every visit on a day, with the unverified and off-plan ones marked. |

### The life of a day

| State | Means | Who moves it next |
|---|---|---|
| <Pill>Proposed</Pill> | You named a city. | He agrees or refuses on his phone. |
| <Pill tone="danger">Refused</Pill> | He said why it will not work. | You — take his city, or propose another. |
| <Pill tone="brand">Agreed</Pill> | He accepted the city. | He picks the shops. |
| <Pill tone="success">Planned</Pill> | He picked at least one shop. | He walks it. |

A salesman can also start a day himself on his phone. It arrives **agreed**, marked *He planned this day himself*.

<Steps>
<Step title="Propose">
On **One salesman → Propose days**, type a city against each day and press **Propose**. He is told *"N days proposed for you"*.
</Step>
<Step title="Answer a refusal">
A refused day shows his reason. Press **take his city** to agree the day on the city he suggested, or type a different city to put back to him. [Today](/docs/sales/today) counts refused days as *Days he will not walk*.
</Step>
<Step title="Let him pick the shops">
Once agreed, he picks the shops on his phone. If a shop has left his book since he picked it, it is dropped and he is told how many were left out.
</Step>
<Step title="Or pick them yourself">
When you have to, **Pick the shops yourself** opens his book for the chosen city. Save a run of up to 31 days at once; a day with no shops in it clears that day.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="note">
You propose a **city**, not shops. He knows which market shuts on a Wednesday and which two towns are too far apart for one day — the reason for asking is that his answer is worth more than a guess from the office.
</Callout>

### Why a visit is unverified

A salesman can only check in to a shop from within <Setting k="mbos.location.visitMismatchM" /> metres of its pin. When the pin is wrong, he can check in anyway by **writing why** — the visit then saves unverified with his words, and he may also say the pin is wrong. That is what you are reading.

| Mark | Means | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| <Pill tone="warn">Unverified</Pill> | Checked in away from the pin, or a check failed. The reason is shown. | Accept it anyway, or ask him to explain. |
| <Pill tone="warn">Pin questioned</Pill> | He says the shop is not where the book has it. | Move the shop's pin here, or leave it. |
| <Pill>Off plan</Pill> | The shop was not on his route for the day. | Read the reason he gave for going. |
| *The visit never closed* | He walked out of signal or did not check out. | — |

<Callout kind="note">
Moving the pin and accepting the visit are separate. Moving it says the book was wrong; accepting says you stand behind the visit. You can do one, both or neither.
</Callout>

## Questions people ask

<Faq q="He refused, and I want him to go anyway.">
There is no overrule. Put the same city back with your reason in a call, or propose another. The refusal is the point of asking.
</Faq>
<Faq q="A day shows 'Agreed, no shops'.">
He accepted the city and has not picked shops yet. Picking nothing leaves the day agreed — an empty day is never shown as a route.
</Faq>
<Faq q="A visit is missing.">
Visits reach the office on the phone's next sync. A salesman with no signal appears later, not never.
</Faq>
<Faq q="I cannot accept a visit.">
You cannot accept your own visits, or visits by somebody outside your team.
</Faq>
