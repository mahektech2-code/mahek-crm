Approvals is the queue of things your salesmen have asked you for and are waiting on: leave, expense claims, tours, corrections to a day's attendance, and requests to work different cities. The oldest is at the top. The person who asked is usually still standing in a shop, so the screen is built to answer quickly — and to make you say why when the answer is no.

You reach it from the **Review** button on [Today](/docs/sales/today). It is not in the sidebar.

## What lands here

| Kind | What he asked for | Where else you see it |
|---|---|---|
| **Leave** | Days off, with the type and whether half a day | [Leave and holidays](/docs/sales/leave-and-holidays) |
| **Expense claim** | Money back for something he paid | [Expenses](/docs/sales/expenses) |
| **Tour** | To work away from base for some days, with cities and an estimated cost | [Field reports](/docs/sales/field-reports) → Tours |
| **Attendance correction** | To fix a day's check-in or check-out | [Attendance](/docs/sales/attendance) |
| **Territory** | To work different cities from the ones he was given | [Territory](/docs/sales/territory) |
| **Order** | An order over the credit limit — **shown here, decided by accounts** | Accounts → Order approvals |
| **Sample**, **Distributor appointment** | Listed if raised, but decided on their own desks | [Lead management](/docs/sales/lead-management) |

## Answering a request

<Steps>
<Step title="Read the row">
*What* is the request in words, read from the thing he asked about — the dates of the leave, the cities of the tour. Hover for any reason he gave.
</Step>
<Step title="Approve, approve part, or refuse">
**Approve** — say anything you want to add, or nothing. **Part** — type the amount you are allowing; it is only offered where the request has an amount. **Refuse** — you must say why.
</Step>
<Step title="Confirm">
The answer goes to his phone at once as a notification, with your note as the message. The row leaves the queue and appears under *Lately decided*.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="warn" title="A refusal needs a reason">
*"Say why. Whoever asked has to be able to do something differently, and they cannot work it out from the word alone."* The confirm button stays disabled until you type one.
</Callout>

## What each answer does

| Answer | Stored as | He is told |
|---|---|---|
| Approve | approved | *Your … request was approved* — with your note, or "… approved it." |
| Part | approved in part, with the amount | *Your … request was approved in part* — with your note |
| Refuse | not approved, with the reason | *Your … request was not approved* — with your reason |

Tapping the notification on his phone opens the screen the request belongs to — his leave, his expenses, his attendance.

## Things the screen will refuse

| You see | Because |
|---|---|
| *This is your own request. Somebody else has to answer it…* | Nobody approves their own leave or expenses. |
| *That request is from somebody outside your team.* | It belongs to another region's manager. |
| *This was already approved on …* | Another manager answered first. The first answer stands. |
| *This leave was withdrawn by the person who asked for it…* | He took it back; there is nothing to decide. |
| *A sample is decided on the samples desk…* | Samples have their own rules about stock and the lead. |
| *Appointing a distributor is a two-step decision with its own screen…* | It needs management as well as you. |
| *A day's expenses are priced and decided on Expenses & claims…* | Day claims have per-line amounts and policy limits. |

## Questions people ask

<Faq q="Approving a request for different cities did not change his cities.">
Approving says yes. You still change his area on [Salesmen](/docs/sales/salesmen) → *Where they work*.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Two of us approved the same leave.">
Only the first answer is saved. The second manager is told it was already decided.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Does a request approve itself if nobody answers?">
No. Nothing is ever approved by a timer. The stale warning is how it gets noticed.
</Faq>
<Faq q="Why can I not see an Approve button on the order row?">
Orders over the credit limit are accounts' decision. The person chasing a target must not sign off the orders that hit it.
</Faq>
